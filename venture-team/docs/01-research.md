# Market Research Report: Smart Glasses in Germany

*Prepared by: Research Agent | Date: 20 November 2026 | Scope: consumer AI/AR smart eyewear, with notes on retail channels and regulation*

> Note on data quality: There is **no public, authoritative unit or revenue figure for German smart-glasses sales**. Germany-specific market sizes come from paid analyst reports whose forecasts differ widely. Global shipment data (Counterpoint, IDC) and German consumer surveys (Bitkom) are the most reliable sources. All estimates are labelled as such.

---

## 1. Market Trends Summary

**Global momentum is very strong, and Western Europe is a major share of it.**
- Counterpoint Research: global smart-glasses shipments grew **110% YoY in H1 2025** and **139% YoY in H2 2025**. AI glasses (camera + assistant, usually no display) were **88%** of H2 2025 shipments.
- In H2 2025, **Western Europe was 30% of global volume**, behind North America (37%), and Counterpoint links this to Meta launches. Average retail price of AI glasses rose to **about US$360**.
- IDC: total XR shipments grew **44.4% in 2025**, driven by smart glasses while VR/MR headsets declined. IDC forecasts **+33.5% in 2026**, mostly from glasses *without* displays.
- Meta reportedly sold **more than 7 million** Ray-Ban/Oakley Meta glasses in 2025 (heise).

**Germany market size (estimates only, treat with caution).**
- MarketsandMarkets (as summarised in search results; the page itself could not be retrieved): Germany smart-glasses market **about US$68 M in 2024 → US$299 M by 2029**.
- Bonafide Research: **>7.7% CAGR 2025–2030**. The gap between these two forecasts shows how uncertain the numbers are. *No figure verified from primary data.*

**2026 is the year displays reached Germany.**
- Even Realities G2 (display, no camera) went on sale in Germany in April 2026 at **€699**.
- Rokid entered Germany on **29 April 2026** (AI Glasses Neo €449; Rokid Glasses with AR display from €699).
- **Meta Ray-Ban Display** launched in Germany on **13 October 2026** at **€899** (with Neural Band), sold via Meta, MediaMarkt and Deutsche Telekom. EU battery and AI rules had delayed it.

**Demand is real, but so is a privacy backlash that is stronger than in other markets.**
- Bitkom survey (published 21 Aug 2026, n = 1,164, aged 16+): **43%** interested in wearing smart glasses (15% have already used one, 28% would like to). **52%** reject them. **76%** do not want to be filmed by them. **62%** would not let a wearer into their home. **82%** want recording to be clearly visible.
- Bitkom's "Future of Consumer Technology 2026" study: **37%** can imagine wearing AR glasses, up from **26%** in the 2025 edition (the question wording differs, so the trend is indicative only).
- Policy direction: the Hamburg data protection commissioner has publicly said a ban is possible. HateAid has filed a criminal complaint against Meta, EssilorLuxottica brands and retailers (including Fielmann). Bitkom rejects blanket bans but calls for visible recording indicators and privacy by design.

## 3. Target Customer Profile

**Context:** **64%** of Germans aged 16+ (**38.7 M people**) wear corrective glasses. The figure is 90% among those 60+ and 29% among 20–29-year-olds (Allensbach/KGS study 2024/25, via ZVA). **86%** wear sunglasses regularly. Prescription support is therefore a basic requirement, not an extra.

**Segment A — Tech-forward young urbans (about 16–39).** *(Inferred from survey and launch data.)*
- Needs: hands-free photos/video, music, AI questions, live translation while travelling.
- Decision factors: style and brand, price (€300–500 range), smartphone integration.
- Caveat: in the Bitkom survey, even 71% of 16–29-year-olds do not want to be filmed. Social acceptance matters to this group too.

**Segment B — Professionals and knowledge workers (about 30–55).**
- Needs: notifications, teleprompter, meeting notes, translation, navigation without taking out a phone.
- Decision factors: discreet look, all-day comfort and battery, **GDPR-compliant data handling**, prescription lenses. Will pay €600–900 for real productivity gains (estimate based on Even G2 / Display pricing).

**Segment C — Glasses wearers 50+ and accessibility users.** *(Opportunity segment, little direct data.)*
- Needs: captions for hard of hearing, reading/vision support, simple voice control, varifocal lenses.
- Decision factors: buying through a trusted optician, simplicity, health benefit. Possible link to statutory health insurance and aids-catalogue reimbursement is **not researched** and would need checking.

**Segment D — B2B / industry (logistics, field service, healthcare).** Germany's strong industrial base suggests demand for "see-what-I-see" remote support and pick-by-vision. *No 2025–2026 German B2B figures found.*

**Cross-segment pain points:** privacy stigma ("am I being filmed?"), battery life, weight, prescription availability/cost, distrust of US/Chinese cloud processing.

## 4. Barriers and Opportunities for Market Entry

### Barriers
1. **Privacy law and public opinion (highest risk).**
   - German law generally prohibits filming identifiable people without consent (Euronews summary).
   - A German regulator view (per MLex) holds that recording people outside close family/friends will "generally" breach GDPR.
   - Hamburg DPA has threatened a ban. HateAid's criminal complaint also targets **retailers**, which may make opticians cautious about stocking camera glasses.
2. **EU Battery Regulation.** Heise (Mar 2026) reports that from **18 Feb 2027** many devices need user-removable/replaceable batteries, which is hard to engineer in glasses. Meta reportedly sought an exemption. *Exact applicability and exemptions for smart glasses need legal review.*
3. **EU AI rules (AI Act) and GDPR for on-device and cloud AI.** These reportedly restrict some AI features (for example vision features that identify people). Meta's EU rollouts were delayed repeatedly.
4. **Standard compliance.** CE marking and the Radio Equipment Directive (including its cybersecurity requirements), WEEE/ElektroG registration, and German-language AI quality. *(Standard requirements, listed without detailed verification.)*
5. **Channel concentration and incumbent dominance.** Meta + EssilorLuxottica control brands, optician relationships and ~80% share. Fielmann sees the category as a niche.

### Opportunities
1. **"Privacy-first by design" positioning.** The Bitkom data shows the gap: 82% want visible recording, 76% don't want to be filmed. Camera-free or privacy-hardened glasses (strong recording indicator, on-device AI, EU data hosting) answer the main objection German consumers have. Even Realities already shows this works.
2. **Optician-native product.** With 38.7 M glasses wearers and opticians cautious about legal risk from camera models, a prescription-first, camera-free product could be an easier listing for Fielmann, Apollo, Rodenstock/Zeiss-linked partners and independent opticians.
3. **Translation, captions and accessibility.** These features are strong use cases in an ageing society and do not need a camera.
4. **B2B / Industrie 4.0.** Workplace use under works-council agreements avoids much of the consumer stigma. *Market size not quantified.*
5. **Timing.** Google/Samsung have not launched in the EU yet. The window before they arrive in Europe (date unknown) is an opening for a regional challenger.
