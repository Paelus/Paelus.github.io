# Strategy & Positioning: Smart Glasses for Germany

*Prepared by: Strategy Agent | Based solely on 01-research.md (20 Nov 2026) | Working brand name: **Sehwerk** (invented)*

---

## 1. Product Positioning

**Positioning statement:**
> **Sehwerk is camera-free, prescription-first AI display glasses, sold through opticians. They give Germany's glasses wearers captions, translation and notes in their field of view without filming anyone, with processing on the device or on EU servers.**

What this means for the product:
- **No camera, by design.** The report names privacy as the biggest barrier in Germany: 76% do not want to be filmed, 62% would not let a wearer into their home, and the Hamburg DPA has floated a ban. Leaving the camera out removes that objection completely. It is a clear statement, not a missing feature.
- **Prescription is the base product, not an extra.** 64% of Germans aged 16+ (38.7 M people) wear corrective glasses. Even Realities charges +€169 for prescription lenses. Sehwerk should include single-vision lenses in the base price and offer varifocals.
- **Data stays in the EU.** On-device processing where possible, EU hosting for the rest, GDPR-first design. This answers the "distrust of US/Chinese cloud processing" pain point found across all segments.
- **Price band: €600–900.** This is the report's estimated willingness to pay for Segment B, and it matches Even G2 (€699) and Meta Ray-Ban Display (€899). The exact price and whether included lenses are affordable at that price: *insufficient information, needs a bill-of-materials and margin study.*

## 2. Target Segments

**Primary: Segment B, professionals and knowledge workers (about 30–55).**
- Why: their needs (notifications, teleprompter, meeting notes, translation, navigation) do not need a camera. They value discretion, GDPR compliance and prescription lenses, and they will pay €600–900 for productivity gains. This is the profile where Sehwerk's strengths count most.
- Proof that it works: Even Realities already sells a camera-free productivity product in Germany at €699.

**Secondary: Segment C, glasses wearers 50+ and accessibility users.**
- Why: 90% of people 60+ wear glasses. Captions for hard-of-hearing users and simple voice control are strong use cases that need no camera, and this group buys through trusted opticians, which is also Sehwerk's channel.
- Caveat: the report has little direct data on this group. Reimbursement by statutory health insurance or listing in the aids catalogue (Hilfsmittelverzeichnis) was **not researched**. *Insufficient information; check before committing to a health-benefit message.*

**Not chosen for launch:**
- **Segment A (young urbans):** they want photos and video, which means a camera, and a €300–500 price, both of which put Sehwerk in a head-on fight with Meta (about 82% global share, from €309).
- **Segment D (B2B / industry):** promising (works-council agreements reduce stigma), but the report has *no German B2B figures*. Review for phase 3 once the market is sized.

## 3. Differentiation Strategy

| Dimension | Meta (Ray-Ban Meta / Display) | Even Realities G2 | Rokid | **Sehwerk** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Camera | Yes, the main target of complaints | No | Yes (Neo) | **No** |
| Prescription | Through EssilorLuxottica opticians | +€169 extra | Up to ±15 dpt (Neo) | **Included; varifocals available** |
| Data handling | Meta cloud | Not specified in report | EU trust concerns | **On-device + EU hosting, GDPR-first** |
| German-language AI | Yes | Not specified in report | Not confirmed | **Required at launch** |
| Retail | Meta, MediaMarkt, Telekom, opticians | Not specified in report | Not specified in report | **Optician-first** |
| Price (DE) | €309–899 | €699 | €449–699+ | **€600–900 (to be validated)** |

**Three selling points to build every message on:**
1. **"Nobody gets filmed."** Camera-free, so wearers can bring it into meetings, homes and clinics without social friction. This answers the Bitkom data and stays out of the HateAid/DPA conflict.
2. **"Your glasses, made smarter."** Prescription and varifocal lenses included, fitted by your optician. This is the gap against Even G2 (extra lens cost) and fits a market where most buyers already wear glasses.
3. **"Your data stays in Europe."** Processing on the device and on EU servers, with German-language AI. This is the gap against US and Chinese competitors.

**The main competitive threat is Even Realities**, which already holds the camera-free position. Sehwerk must win on included prescription, optician distribution, EU data handling and German-language quality, not on being camera-free alone. Whether Even's own data handling and German AI are weaker: *insufficient information in the report; needs a teardown and hands-on test.*

## 4. Go-to-Market Plan (GTM)

**Phase 0: Pre-launch (now to launch, before 18 Feb 2027 if possible)**
- **Regulatory clearance first:** legal review of the EU Battery Regulation (replaceable batteries from 18 Feb 2027), AI Act limits, CE/RED cybersecurity and WEEE/ElektroG registration. A non-compliant battery design would block the launch, so this goes first.
- **Prove privacy claims:** independent GDPR/data-protection audit, published openly. Talk early with Bitkom, which supports privacy-by-design and has rejected blanket bans.
- **German-language AI quality testing** for captions, translation and notes.

**Phase 1: Optician pilot (first 6 months after launch)**
- **Channel:** 30–50 independent opticians plus approaching one chain (Apollo, or the Fielmann smart-glasses stores, about 200 of ~1,300 locations). Pitch: *"No camera, so no legal risk for you as a retailer"*. HateAid's complaint also names retailers, so this argument is directly relevant to them.
- **Give opticians a reason to stock it:** fitting training, demo frames, a margin on lens fitting, and an easy returns process.
- **Target users:** Segment B in large cities. Use the pilot to measure conversion, returns, battery satisfaction and weight complaints.
- **Content marketing:** demo videos of meeting notes, teleprompter and live translation. Earned media on "the smart glasses you may wear anywhere".

**Phase 2: Scale and Segment C (months 6–18)**
- Expand to more chains depending on phase-1 results.
- Launch a captions and accessibility version for 50+ users through opticians and, if approved, audiologists/hearing-aid partners. *Health-insurance reimbursement must be researched first.*
- Add online direct sales with an optician fitting appointment (online order, local fitting).
- Consider electronics retail (MediaMarkt, Telekom) only after the optician channel works. These channels already push Meta's €899 Display, so shelf competition there would be direct.

**Phase 3: Optional B2B pilot (after month 18)**
- Test field service or healthcare with one or two industrial partners under works-council agreements. Size this market first, since the report has *no German B2B data*.

**Timing advantage:** Google/Samsung Android XR glasses have no EU launch date. Sehwerk should establish the "privacy-first, optician-sold" position before they arrive.

**KPIs to track (targets: insufficient information, set after the pilot):** stocking opticians, sell-through per store, return rate, share of prescription/varifocal orders, press tone on privacy.

**Key open questions for follow-up research:** unit economics with included lenses; Battery Regulation applicability; health-insurance reimbursement; how strong Even Realities is in German retail; German B2B demand.
