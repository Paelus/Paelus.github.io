# Marketing Copy: CampusLoop

*Based solely on `02-strategy.md`. Primary reader: catering and sustainability managers at Studierendenwerke, so the tone is professional and factual. Student-facing lines appear only where the strategy plans student activation (pilot campuses, with AStA and sustainability offices). Open points the strategy marks as "insufficient information" (price per cup, campus card compatibility, exact PPWR reporting requirements) are not promised here.*

## Slogan

**Your brand. Their student card. Every CampusLoop campus.**

## Website Copy

### Hero

**Headline:** Reusable cups that work: on the student card, under your brand.

**Subheadline:** CampusLoop is a white-label cup deposit system for Studierendenwerke. The deposit is booked on the student card, cups can be returned at any participating campus, and you pay per cup instead of a monthly system fee.

**Supporting line:** Single-use cups are getting more expensive in university towns with a packaging tax, and the PPWR requires a reusable option from about February 2028. CampusLoop helps you run a system that is actually used, not one that only exists on paper.

### Key Benefits

**1. No app, no cash, no queue at the till.**
Students pay the deposit (proposed: €1, in line with the usual campus deposit) with the card they already carry. There is no registration and no app download, which is a hurdle for spontaneous coffee buyers. Campus-card deposits have already worked in practice, for example at StuCup in Munich. *Integration with your card system is checked together with you before any pilot starts.*

**2. Pay per cup, not per month.**
No monthly minimum and no fixed system fee: you pay a low fee per cup used. Quiet cafeterias do not pay for capacity they do not need. *The exact per-cup price is set during the pilot, based on real washing and logistics costs.*

**3. One return network across campuses.**
A cup borrowed in Tübingen can be returned in Konstanz. CampusLoop links participating Studierendenwerke through a shared clearing service, so you are not running an island solution. Already running your own system? We connect it rather than replace it.

**4. Your brand stays on the cup.**
CampusLoop is white label: cups, return points and communication carry the Studierendenwerk's name. If you prefer to keep your own cups, you can use our software on its own.

**5. Built-in nudges and the data you need.**
We provide cashier settings for a reusable discount or a single-use surcharge. Discounts were the most effective nudge in the HWR Berlin survey, and they are legally allowed. You also get usage data to support your VerpackG reporting and to track progress towards the 2030 reuse target.

## Pitch Deck Key Points

1. **Problem:** The Mehrwegangebotspflicht has applied since 2023 but is poorly enforced. Reusable cups are on offer, but rarely used.
2. **Problem:** Costs are rising. Packaging taxes (Tübingen €0.50 per cup, Konstanz, Freiburg since January 2026) and the PPWR (a reusable option from about February 2028, a 10% reuse target by 2030) put Studierendenwerke under pressure to act.
3. **Problem:** Today's options do not fit campuses. App systems put off spontaneous buyers, monthly fees put off low-volume outlets, and in-house systems stop at the campus boundary.
4. **Solution:** CampusLoop is a white-label cup deposit system for Studierendenwerke: deposit on the student card, return bins next to existing dish-return points, and cross-campus clearing.
5. **Differentiation:** It is the only option in our comparison that combines campus-card deposit, no app, payment per cup and a return network across all participating campuses, under the Studierendenwerk's own brand.
6. **Differentiation:** We connect existing systems instead of replacing them, and we offer our software without our cups.
7. **Benefit:** Higher uptake through standard price nudges (a reusable discount or single-use surcharge) and staff who actively offer the cup.
8. **Benefit and next step:** A paid pilot with 2-4 cafeterias produces the first campus data on return rate, cycles per cup (goal: more than 25, well above the UBA break-even of about 10) and cost per cycle, ready for the board and for compliance reporting.
