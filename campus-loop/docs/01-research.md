# Market Research Report: Reusable Cup Deposit Systems for German University Campuses

*Research date: November 2026. Figures are from public sources (2019-2026); older sources are labelled as such. "Estimate" means our own derivation, not a published number.*

## 1. Market Trends Summary

**The law requires a reusable option, but it is barely enforced and little used.**
- Since 1 January 2023 the **Mehrwegangebotspflicht** (§§ 33-34 VerpackG) has required caterers that sell takeaway drinks and food to offer a reusable alternative. It must not cost more than the single-use version, though a deposit is allowed. Small outlets (≤ 5 staff and ≤ 80 m²) may instead fill customers' own cups. ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/umwelt-haushalt/abfall/mehrwegangebotspflicht-fuer-essen-und-getraenke-zum-mitnehmen-79833))
- **Compliance is weak.** Between 2023 and 2025 the Deutsche Umwelthilfe (DUH) tested **208 outlets of 52 chains** and found violations at **116**. In its latest round (Dec 2025), **18 of 32** bakery and petrol-station outlets broke the rules. In 208 visits, staff never once offered reusables unprompted. The DUH calls the obligation "largely ineffective" and says state authorities hardly check it. The DUH has won 22 court cases and lost one. ([DUH, 30.12.2025](https://www.duh.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/mehrwegboykott-auch-nach-drei-jahren-angebotspflicht-deutsche-umwelthilfe-deckt-massive-verstoesse-be/); [DUH test table](https://www.duh.de/fileadmin/user_upload/download/Pressemitteilungen/Kreislaufwirtschaft/251230_Tabelle_Tests_Mehrwegangebotspflicht.pdf)) *Caveat: these are NGO spot checks, not official statistics. We found no official enforcement data from the federal states.*
- **Usage is low.** A GVM study for WWF counted 13.7 billion takeaway packages in German food service in 2022, 2.2 billion of them for drinks, with only about 0.74% reusable. In 2023 the reusable share for **beverages roughly doubled to 7.0%**, but growth then stalled after a few months ([WWF/GVM](https://www.wwf.de/fileadmin/fm-wwf/Publikationen-PDF/Plastik/Mehrweg-in-der-deutschen-Gastronomie-1-Jahr-Mehrwegangebotspflicht.pdf)). *We found no public to-go figure for 2024 or 2025.*

**Pressure on single-use packaging is rising:**
- **Municipal packaging taxes.** The Federal Constitutional Court upheld Tübingen's tax of €0.50 per single-use cup or container (decision of 27 Nov 2024, published 22 Jan 2025) ([BVerfG](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2025/bvg25-006.html)). Konstanz has a similar tax, and Freiburg's started on 1 Jan 2026 ([DUH](https://www.duh.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/mehrwegboykott-auch-nach-drei-jahren-angebotspflicht-deutsche-umwelthilfe-deckt-massive-verstoesse-be/)). Many of these are university towns. The IHK Oldenburg puts the time to introduce such a tax at about 1.5 years ([IHK](https://www.ihk.de/oldenburg/geschaeftsfelder/innovation-energie-umwelt/umwelt-und-umweltschutz/abfall-und-kreislaufwirtschaft2/kommunale-verpackungssteuer-6536546)).
- **EU Packaging Regulation (PPWR, Reg. (EU) 2025/40).**
  - From about Feb 2027, takeaway outlets must let customers fill their own containers (Art. 32).
  - From Feb 2028, they must offer a reusable option, with a **10% reuse target by 2030** (Art. 33).
  - Secondary sources disagree on the exact dates, so check them against the Official Journal. ([WKO](https://www.wko.at/tourismus-freizeitwirtschaft/gastronomie/eu-verpackungsverordnung-ppwr); [Bundesrat 98/26](https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2026/0098-26B.pdf); [PPWRConnect](https://ppwrconnect.com/de/blog/ppwr-reusable-packaging-food-service-takeaway))
- **Political demands.** The DUH calls for a national levy on single-use packaging and for extending the reusable obligation in 2027.

**Takeaway:** the rules keep getting stricter, but people are not changing their habits on their own. The gap is in making reusables convenient and in convincing operators to use them, not in awareness.

## 2. Key Competitors

| Player | Model | Positioning / strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RECUP / REBOWL** (Munich) | Cash deposit: €1 per cup, €5 per bowl. Partners pay a system fee and handle the deposit. | Market leader, about **20,000-21,000 outlets** (company figures, reported from 2023-2024; [press kit 04/2024](https://recup.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Pressekit-RECUP-REBOWL-1-1.pdf), [DUH overview](https://www.duh.de/informieren/ressourcen-und-abfall/to-go-verpackungen/becherheld/mehrwegsysteme-coffee-to-go/)). Simple for guests, no app needed. Owns its cup manufacturer, Crafting Future, since 2023 ([Munich Startup](https://munich-startup.de/news/recup-uebernimmt-mehrweg-hersteller-crafting-future)). | Monthly fees put off low-volume outlets. A 2019 example from Ulm cites €30 per month and almost no demand ([Schwäbische, 2019](https://www.schwaebische.de/regional/ulm-alb-donau/ulm/recup-becher-kommen-schlechter-an-als-erwartet-240028)). |

Venture-funded, now focusing on events ([Meininger](https://www.meininger.de/gastronomie/events/vytal-sammelt-millionen-fuer-digitale-mehrwegbecher)). | Needs an app and registration, which is a barrier for spontaneous coffee buyers. The reasons were not published. |
| **In-house / machine systems at Studierendenwerke** | StuCup Munich: €1 deposit booked on the student card, return machine ([LMU, 2020](https://www.lmu.de/de/newsroom/newsuebersicht/news/mehrwegsystem-stucup-startet-in-die-pilotphase-8b7f9c25.html)). LogiCUP Bonn: €0.50 deposit with return machine ([StW Bonn flyer, 2019](https://fachschaftmedizin.uni-bonn.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/2019-11_LogiCup_Einfuhrung_C-ampus-Venusberg_Flyer_StwB.pdf)). | Built into campus card payment, no third-party fees. | Island solutions: cups cannot be returned off campus. Tübingen-Hohenheim has had no single-use packaging since 2022 ([Uni Tübingen](https://uni-tuebingen.de/universitaet/aktuelles-und-publikationen/attempto-online/archiv-attempto-online/archivfullview-attempto/article/gemeinsam-gegen-die-einwegflut-1/)). | Cheapest option. | Depends on people remembering their own cup. |

Other pool providers exist (e.g. FairCup, Rebowl variants, regional systems; see the [DUH provider list](https://www.duh.de/informieren/ressourcen-und-abfall/to-go-verpackungen/becherheld/mehrwegsysteme-coffee-to-go/)). For a new entrant, the realistic competitors are **RECUP**, which is widely established, and **Studierendenwerke building their own systems**.

## 3. Target Customer Profile

**Buyer: Studierendenwerke (B2B/B2G decision-makers).**
- **Size:** 57 Studierendenwerke run **865 Mensen and cafeterias** with about 229,000 seats and roughly **€432 million in catering revenue** ([DSW Zahlenspiegel 2025/26](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/fileadmin/user_upload/261113_DSW_Zsp2526_final_.pdf)).
- **Example of volume:** Studentenwerk München alone served about **1 million hot drinks a year** ([LMU, 2020](https://www.lmu.de/de/newsroom/newsuebersicht/news/mehrwegsystem-stucup-startet-in-die-pilotphase-8b7f9c25.html)).
- **Estimate:** if that ratio held roughly across all 57, the national total would be in the tens of millions of hot drinks a year. This is not a published figure.
- **Decision factors:**
  - legal compliance (VerpackG now, PPWR from 2028)
  - **cost per use**: they are non-profit, price-sensitive and under subsidy pressure, and the DSW cites a €4 billion renovation backlog for Mensen ([DSW](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/mensa-warum-sie-politisch-so-wichtig-ist))
  - hygiene and dishwashing logistics
  - integration with the campus card
  - public procurement rules
- **Pain points:**
  - third-party fees on low-margin coffee
  - lost cups
  - extra dishwashing work
  - app barriers for guests
  - Saarland shows they will switch provider or build their own system if a pool system does not fit

**Users: students and staff.**
- **Size:** about 2.8-2.9 million students in Germany (*Destatis order of magnitude, not re-checked for WS 2025/26*), plus university staff.
- **Price sensitivity:** high. €0.50-1.00 deposits are the established norm on campus (Bonn €0.50; Munich and RECUP €1).
- **Behaviour:**
  - Price incentives work better than appeals. In an HWR Berlin survey (n=102, aged 20-30, hypothetical), a **discount on reusables was the most effective nudge** ([HWR Berlin, 2024](https://hwr-berlin.de/fileadmin/portal/Dokumente/Aktuelles/Neuigkeiten/2024/Saba_Feilbach_.pdf)).
  - Returns depend on how convenient they are. In the "Mehrweg Modell Stadt" pilot (Wiesbaden, 2024), about 78% of cups came back to the same company and 68% to the same outlet ([Wiesbaden PIWI](https://piwi.wiesbaden.de/dokument/v/3338566)).
  - The REPAID study (IÖW/ifeu/Recup/Vytal, Jan 2025) found that logistics and hygiene rules make returns harder, and it recommends public return machines ([idw](https://idw-online.de/de/news845858)).
- **Not found:** we found **no public data on how often students forget or keep campus cups**, or on campus return rates. This must be measured in a pilot.

## 4. Barriers and Opportunities for Market Entry

**Barriers**
1. **Incumbent and in-house options.** RECUP is already well known with more than 20,000 outlets. Studierendenwerke can and do build their own systems (Munich, Bonn, Saarland).
2. **Procurement and decision cycles.** Public tendering and boards make sales cycles long. *Our estimate: 6-18 months, not verified.*
3. **Unit economics.** Coffee margins are thin. Fees of €0.10-0.30 per use, or monthly licences, are hard to justify when uptake is low (see the Ulm and Saarland cases).
4. **Logistics and hygiene.** Washing, return points and machine maintenance are needed. The REPAID study names these as the main barriers to returns.
5. **Low voluntary demand.** Only about 7% of takeaway drinks were in reusables in 2023, and enforcement is weak.

**Opportunities**
1. **Regulatory tailwind.** The PPWR reusable offer (2028) and the 10% target (2030) apply EU-wide, and more municipal packaging taxes are following the 2025 court ruling. Single-use cups are getting more expensive for caterers.
2. **Campus card integration.** Booking the deposit on the student card removes cash and app hurdles, and StuCup shows it works. This is a gap the national pool providers do not cover by default.
3. **A closed, repeat-use environment.** Students return to the same buildings daily, which suits high return rates and many cycles per cup. UBA says cups break even ecologically after about 10 cycles and do better above 25 (cited in [LMU](https://www.lmu.de/de/newsroom/newsuebersicht/news/mehrwegsystem-stucup-startet-in-die-pilotphase-8b7f9c25.html)).
4. **Switching moment.** Saarland's exit from Relevo shows Studierendenwerke are re-tendering. A low-fee, white-label system that can work across campuses (shared return between Studierendenwerke) could win them.
5. **Price incentive design.** A small single-use surcharge or a reusable discount is legally allowed and is the most effective lever according to the student survey.
