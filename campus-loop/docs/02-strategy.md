# Strategy & Positioning: Reusable Cups for German Campuses

*Based solely on `01-research.md` (November 2026). Working brand name: **CampusLoop** (invented; it does not match any provider named in the research report). Claims marked "insufficient information" need further research.*

## 1. Product Positioning

**Positioning statement:**
> CampusLoop is a low-fee, white-label cup deposit system for Studierendenwerke: the deposit is booked on the student card, cups can be returned at any participating campus, and the Studierendenwerk keeps its own brand. It removes the app barrier of Vytal/Relevo and the fixed monthly fees of RECUP.

**What we are:** a system, not a cup brand. We provide the cup pool, the campus-card deposit link, return logistics and a cross-campus clearing service, sold B2B/B2G to Studierendenwerke.

**What we are not:** a city-wide pool for every café (RECUP covers that with about 20,000+ outlets), and not an app-based loan scheme.

**Why now (from the report):**
- The Mehrwegangebotspflicht has applied since 2023 but is poorly enforced. PPWR requires a reusable option from about Feb 2028 and sets a 10% reuse target by 2030. Studierendenwerke will need a system that works, not just one that exists on paper.
- Municipal packaging taxes (Tübingen €0.50, Konstanz, Freiburg since Jan 2026) make single-use cups more expensive, especially in university towns.
- Saarland's switch from Relevo to its own system (April 2025) shows Studierendenwerke are willing to re-tender.

## 2. Target Segments

**Primary segment: Studierendenwerke in cities with a packaging tax or one under discussion.**
- *Why:* they face the most direct cost pressure on single-use cups (€0.50 per cup in Tübingen), so the business case is easiest to make.
- *Starting list:* Tübingen, Konstanz, Freiburg (named in the report). Which other cities are planning a tax: **insufficient information; we should check municipal council agendas.** The IHK estimate of about 1.5 years to introduce a tax gives us a lead-time window.

**Secondary segment: Studierendenwerke that run or recently left a system.**
- *Why:* they already have dishwashing processes and data, and they are open to change. Examples are Saarland (own system since 2025), Munich (StuCup) and Bonn (LogiCUP).
- *Our offer to them:* do not replace their system; connect it. Cross-campus return and shared clearing fix the "island solution" weakness named in the report.
- *Risk:* Saarland built its own system and may not want an outside provider. Why it left Relevo was not published (**insufficient information**). We should interview them before pitching.

**End users (not buyers): students and staff.** They are price-sensitive and used to €0.50-1.00 deposits. We design for them but do not sell to them.

**Deprioritised:** private campus cafés and small outlets. The report shows low-volume outlets drop monthly fees (Ulm), and small outlets can legally just fill customers' own cups.

## 3. Differentiation Strategy

| Dimension | RECUP | Vytal / Relevo | In-house (StuCup, LogiCUP, Pfand-tastisch) | **CampusLoop** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guest access | Cash deposit, €1 | App + registration | Campus card or cash | **Campus card, no app** |
| Operator cost | Monthly system fee | €0.10-0.30 per use, Relevo setup €99-499 | Own investment in machines and staff | **Low per-cup fee, no monthly minimum** |
| Return network | Large, city-wide | App-based | Own campus only | **All participating campuses** |
| Branding | RECUP brand | Provider brand | Own brand | **Studierendenwerk's own brand (white label)** |

**Our selling points:**
1. **No app, no cash.** The deposit (proposed: €1, in line with the campus norm) is booked on the student card, as StuCup has shown works. This tackles the app barrier the report identifies for spontaneous coffee buyers.
2. **Pay per cup, no monthly fee.** This responds directly to the fee resistance seen in Ulm and to the €0.10-0.30 per-use costs of app systems. Our exact price point: **insufficient information.** We have no data on our own washing and logistics costs, so it must be set during the pilot.
3. **Cross-campus return.** A student can return a Tübingen cup in Konstanz. This is the gap between national pools (no card integration) and in-house systems (island solutions).
4. **Built-in price nudge.** We provide the cashier settings for a single-use surcharge or reusable discount. The HWR Berlin survey found discounts were the most effective nudge, and they are legally allowed.
5. **Compliance reporting.** We give operators usage data for VerpackG/PPWR reporting and the 2030 10% target. What reporting PPWR will actually require: **insufficient information; check the Official Journal text.**

**What we will not try to compete on:** network size off campus (RECUP wins there) or food bowls in the first phase (Vytal's strength).

## 4. Go-to-Market Plan (GTM)

**Phase 0: Validation (months 0-3)**
- Interview 5-8 Studierendenwerke catering managers, including Saarland, Tübingen and Munich. Ask about switching reasons, dishwashing capacity, campus card systems and procurement rules.
- Find out which campus card systems are in use and how hard integration is. **Insufficient information in the report; this is a key technical risk.**
- Clarify procurement: under what threshold can a pilot be awarded without a full tender? (The report's 6-18 month sales cycle is an unverified estimate.)

**Phase 1: Paid pilot at 1-2 Studierendenwerke (months 3-12)**
- Target: one tax-city Studierendenwerk (e.g. Tübingen) plus one with an existing system that wants cross-campus return.
- Scope: 2-4 cafeterias, campus-card deposit, return bins next to existing dish-return points (the REPAID study recommends public return points).
- KPIs to measure, since the report found **no public data on campus return rates**: return rate, cycles per cup (goal: more than 25, above the UBA break-even of about 10), share of hot drinks in reusables (benchmark: 7.0% national beverage share in 2023), cost per cycle.

**Phase 2: Regional cluster (months 12-24)**
- Expand to neighbouring Studierendenwerke in the same region to make cross-campus return real (e.g. Baden-Württemberg: Tübingen, Konstanz, Freiburg).
- Publish pilot results as a case study for the next tenders.

**Phase 3: National roll-out ahead of PPWR (months 24-36, i.e. before Feb 2028)**
- Pitch the remaining Studierendenwerke (57 in total, 865 Mensen and cafeterias) with pilot data and a PPWR compliance package.

**Channels**
- **Deutsches Studierendenwerk (DSW) and its meetings** for catering managers: the most direct route to all 57 buyers. Which events exist and when: **insufficient information.**
- **Direct sales** to catering heads, backed by the pilot case study.
- **Student-facing activation** on pilot campuses with AStA and sustainability offices: launch weeks with a reusable discount, cup posters at checkouts. Staff should actively offer the cup, since the DUH found staff never offered reusables unprompted.
- **Policy content:** short briefs on packaging taxes and PPWR deadlines for Studierendenwerk boards.

**Main risks**
- A Studierendenwerk builds its own system again (as Saarland did). Mitigation: white label plus an option to buy our software without our cups.
- Low uptake if single-use stays cheap. Mitigation: make the price nudge a standard part of every contract.
- Long procurement cycles. Mitigation: start with small pilots and plan cash runway for 18 months.
